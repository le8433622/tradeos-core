# HUMAN_NATURE_DATA_MODEL.md

Canonical source: `FOUNDER_THINKING.md`.

Deep axiom layer: `HUMAN_NATURE_AXIOMS.md`.

This document defines the human-nature operating model behind TradeOS.

The product does not wait for perfect production data to understand core human behavior. Human behavior in trade has repeated for thousands of years. Real-world data calibrates details. Evidence validates outcomes. But the baseline model comes from stable human nature.

## Core Doctrine

Human nature is the invariant behavioral dataset.

Sandbox scenarios can be valid when they model timeless human drives:

- greed and wanting advantage;
- fear of loss;
- confusion under uncertainty;
- pride and status pressure;
- doubt and distrust;
- urgency and impatience;
- avoidance of responsibility;
- desire for control;
- need for social proof;
- tendency to trust signals, brands, familiarity, and authority.

These patterns are older than software, platforms, APIs, marketplaces, and AI models.

## The Five Human Roots

Use these roots as behavioral priors when designing workflows, scoring risk, testing recommendations, and evaluating AI influence.

### 1. Tham - Wanting Gain

People want lower price, better terms, faster delivery, higher margin, and advantage over alternatives.

Product implications:

- cheap options must be checked for hidden risk;
- recommendations must disclose why cheap may be dangerous;
- negotiation workflows must preserve evidence;
- AI must not exploit greed with fake discounts or pressure.

### 2. San - Aversion And Reactivity

People react strongly to delay, disrespect, perceived loss, supplier excuses, and betrayal.

Product implications:

- disputes need evidence trails;
- supplier communication should be calm and auditable;
- refund, delay, and quality problems need human review;
- AI must not intensify conflict for engagement.

### 3. Si - Confusion And Misperception

People misunderstand tradeoffs, overtrust weak signals, miss hidden costs, and confuse activity with progress.

Product implications:

- recommendations must show alternatives and missing proof;
- AI must mark uncertainty;
- decision tables should reduce noise;
- dashboards are useful only when they improve decisions.

### 4. Man - Pride, Status, And Face

People protect reputation, avoid admitting uncertainty, prefer being seen as capable, and may reject good advice if it threatens status.

Product implications:

- workflows should help users stay in control;
- copy must not shame users;
- approvals should preserve dignity;
- AI should strengthen judgment, not replace it.

### 5. Nghi - Doubt And Distrust

People doubt suppliers, platforms, AI recommendations, prices, quality, and motives.

Product implications:

- evidence is mandatory;
- every claim should be traceable;
- missing proof should be visible;
- trust must be earned by recorded work, not asserted.

## Sandbox Is Valid When It Models Human Nature

Sandbox data is not fake if it accurately models stable human behavior and economic incentives.

Sandbox scenarios are valid for:

- testing decision confusion;
- testing greed-driven cheap-price selection;
- testing distrust and evidence gaps;
- testing high-risk supplier behavior;
- testing approval boundaries;
- testing AI manipulation risks;
- testing platform failure fallback;
- testing buyer willingness logic at the scenario level.

Sandbox is insufficient only when the question is about exact market price, exact supplier availability, exact buyer willingness to pay, production reliability, or legal/compliance facts.

## Real Data Role

Real data is still required for:

- evidence claims;
- production price accuracy;
- supplier verification;
- actual payment validation;
- actual buyer demand;
- delivery, refund, and dispute outcomes;
- operational reliability;
- legal or compliance claims.

Real data calibrates and validates. It does not replace the human-nature baseline.

## Design Rule

When production data is absent, design from human nature, manual workflow, and sandbox scenarios.

When production data exists, use it to calibrate thresholds, verify outcomes, and price delivered work.

Do not let "we need real data" become an excuse for avoiding obvious human-risk design.

Do not let sandbox confidence become a claim about facts that require evidence.

## Agent Rule

Coding agents must treat human nature as a valid source of scenario design.

Before asking for more data, determine whether the missing information is:

1. A human-behavior prior already known from stable human nature.
2. A market fact that needs evidence.
3. A production reliability fact that needs testing.
4. A paid-proof fact that needs actual buyer payment.

Only items 2, 3, and 4 require real-world data. Item 1 can be modeled with sandbox scenarios if the assumptions are stated.

## Final Rule

Human nature is the baseline dataset.
Evidence proves specific claims.
Cashflow validates product value.
