# ANTI_MANIPULATION_POLICY.md

Canonical source: `FOUNDER_THINKING.md`.

This policy is not abstract ethics. It is an operating rule: AI influence must make the user stronger, not more dependent, confused, pressured, or easier to monetize.

This policy is grounded in stable human nature: wanting gain, fearing loss, confusion, pride/status pressure, doubt, distrust, urgency, and desire for control.

## 1. Core Principle

AI influence is unavoidable.
The product must ensure influence increases human strength, not dependency.

The system must never exploit confusion, fear, greed, shame, urgency, loneliness, or lack of knowledge to push users toward actions that mainly benefit the system.

The system must especially avoid manipulation around money, supplier selection, legal commitment, final order decisions, refund/dispute risk, and urgency.

The same human drives that create buyer pain can be exploited. TradeOS must use them to protect the user, not to pressure the user.

---

## 2. Care vs Manipulation

Care:

- transparent;
- evidence-based;
- reversible;
- consent-based;
- increases user capability;
- expands choices;
- protects against loss.

Manipulation:

- hides intent;
- narrows choices;
- exploits emotion;
- reduces reflection;
- creates dependency;
- benefits the system at the user's expense;
- weakens user decision quality.

---

## 3. Forbidden Patterns

Do not implement:

- dark patterns;
- fake urgency;
- hidden fees;
- misleading recommendations;
- emotional pressure copy;
- addictive loops;
- biased ranking without disclosure;
- AI pretending certainty where there is uncertainty;
- nudges that bypass user judgment;
- automatic commitment without approval.
- ranking that hides evidence quality;
- recommendation copy that pressures payment before delivered work is proven;
- automation that removes review from money, legal, reputation, or irreversible decisions.

---

## 4. Required Patterns

Every high-impact workflow must include:

- clear explanation;
- uncertainty disclosure;
- alternatives;
- evidence;
- human approval;
- ability to cancel;
- ability to edit;
- audit trail;
- reason log.
- missing proof flags when evidence is incomplete;
- manual fallback when platform data is unavailable;
- clear distinction between AI recommendation and human commitment.

---

## 5. Human Strength Metrics

Track whether the system improves:

- decision clarity;
- time saved;
- money saved;
- risk avoided;
- options understood;
- successful approvals;
- dispute reduction;
- repeat value;
- user ability to make better future decisions.

Do not optimize only for clicks, engagement, or conversion.

Optimize for accountable economic value:

- buyer loss reduced;
- evidence produced;
- risk avoided;
- successful human approvals;
- delivered-work units completed;
- cashflow backed by proof.

---

## 6. Approval Boundary Rule

Never use UX, ranking, copy, or automation to soften the approval boundary.

Human approval is required for:

- money movement;
- final order confirmation;
- legal commitment;
- supplier contract acceptance;
- irreversible action;
- high-risk negotiation;
- suspicious supplier behavior;
- ambiguous tradeoffs.

The AI may do the labor. The human owns the commitment.
