# FOUNDER_THINKING.md - Product Soul Source Of Truth

This file is the canonical founder-thinking document for TradeOS.

Every coding agent, product agent, AI vendor, reviewer, and operator must internalize this before writing or modifying code. The job is not only to implement tasks. The job is to preserve the product soul.

## Core Sentence

This product is not built to look intelligent.
It is built to create accountable economic value.

AI does the labor.
Human approves the commitment.
Evidence proves the work.
Cashflow validates the product.

Human nature is the invariant behavioral dataset.

Deep axiom layer: `HUMAN_NATURE_AXIOMS.md`.

---

## 1. This Is Not Ordinary Software

This project is not a normal app with a UI, CRUD, dashboard, chatbot, marketplace, CRM, or workflow shell.

It is an AI-native economic operator.

The system exists to help buyers reduce real loss:

- money loss;
- time loss;
- trust loss;
- supplier risk;
- safety risk;
- reputation risk;
- operational disruption;
- decision confusion;
- wrong purchase decisions;
- fraud exposure.

If the product looks intelligent but does not help buyers reduce loss, reduce risk, save time, increase trust, or make better decisions, it fails.

---

## 2. Buyer Value Comes First

Every feature must start from one question:

What is the buyer losing, fearing, or stuck on, and are they willing to pay to avoid that loss?

Do not start from:

- AI capability;
- dashboard ideas;
- marketplace imagination;
- generic workflow models;
- provider complaints;
- technical cleverness;
- available API features.

Start from real buyer loss.

Real buyer loss comes from stable human behavior: wanting gain, fearing loss, confusion, pride, doubt, distrust, status pressure, urgency, and avoidance of responsibility. These patterns are older than any platform or dataset.

---

## 2.1 Human Nature Is The Baseline Dataset

The system does not need perfect production data to understand core human behavior. Human behavior in trade has repeated for thousands of years.

Use human nature as the baseline behavioral dataset:

- tham - wanting gain, lower price, advantage, margin;
- san - aversion, anger, reactivity to loss or betrayal;
- si - confusion, misperception, noisy decision-making;
- man - pride, status, face, reputation pressure;
- nghi - doubt, distrust, suspicion, fear of being deceived.

Sandbox data is valid when it models these stable human patterns and economic incentives.

Real-world data is still required for exact market facts, supplier verification, operational reliability, legal claims, and actual paid proof. But real data calibrates and validates; it does not replace the human-nature baseline.

Do not let "we need real data" become an excuse for ignoring obvious human-risk design. Do not let sandbox confidence become a factual claim without evidence.

See `HUMAN_NATURE_AXIOMS.md` and `HUMAN_NATURE_DATA_MODEL.md`.

---

## 3. AI Is An Operator, Not A Chatbot

AI in this system must perform real work.

AI may:

- source products;
- find suppliers;
- message suppliers;
- request quotes;
- collect quote replies;
- compare options;
- detect risk;
- identify missing proof;
- prepare decision tables;
- preserve evidence;
- recommend next actions;
- draft orders;
- request human approval;
- follow up;
- record outcomes.

AI does the repetitive, time-consuming, information-noisy labor.
Humans keep control over commitment.

---

## 4. Human-In-The-Money-Loop

AI must not independently:

- move money;
- place final orders;
- sign contracts;
- accept legal commitments;
- execute irreversible actions;
- decide high-risk tradeoffs alone;
- hide uncertainty;
- fabricate evidence.

Rule:

AI does the labor.
Human approves the commitment.
The system records who approved what, when, and based on what evidence.

Every workflow touching money, legal exposure, reputation, or irreversible action must include a human approval checkpoint.

---

## 5. Evidence Ledger Is The Core Of Trust

The system must preserve the process, not only the result.

Evidence should record:

- user objective;
- initial request;
- selection criteria;
- data sources;
- links and screenshot references;
- supplier identities;
- messages sent;
- replies received;
- quotes;
- comparison tables;
- risk scores;
- recommendation rationale;
- AI confidence;
- approval record;
- executor and timestamp;
- invoice;
- delivery confirmation;
- dispute or refund outcome.

No evidence means no trust.
No trust means no durable cashflow.

---

## 6. Pay By Delivered Work

Do not prioritize vague subscription logic before paid proof.

Prefer pay-by-delivered-work:

- one paid sourcing request;
- one supplier comparison table;
- one supplier verification;
- one quote collection batch;
- one negotiation batch across 20-100 shops;
- one risk report;
- one dispute support case;
- one cheaper-alternative search;
- one reorder optimization;
- one decision report.

If delivered work is not proven, the system should not demand payment for it.

---

## 7. Do Not Build Generic Apps

Do not let this project drift into:

- generic CRM;
- generic ERP;
- generic marketplace;
- generic chatbot;
- generic shopping app;
- generic management dashboard;
- AI that talks but does not create evidence-backed value.

Each module must answer:

- What loss does it reduce?
- Who pays?
- Why do they pay at that moment?
- What real work does AI perform?
- What evidence is produced?
- Where does the user approve?
- If it is wrong, who carries the risk?
- What should not be built yet?

---

## 8. Manual First, Then Automation

Do not automate what is not understood manually.

Correct sequence:

1. Make the manual workflow work.
2. Capture evidence.
3. Find repeatable patterns.
4. Standardize registered actions.
5. Let AI orchestrate only existing safe actions.

Manual flow is the source of truth.
Automation is an orchestration layer.
AI must not violate policy.

---

## 9. Platform Access Is Leverage, Not Oxygen

The system must not depend on one platform API, ranking, account permission, or goodwill.

If an API is blocked, the workflow must still operate through:

- user-provided links;
- screenshots;
- uploaded invoices;
- manual catalogs;
- chat quotes;
- internal forms;
- operator-entered data;
- browser workflows;
- email, Zalo, WhatsApp, or manual follow-up;
- multiple substitute data sources.

Platform access is leverage, not oxygen.

---

## 10. No Intelligence Theater

The goal is not optimized intelligence.
The goal is accountable economic value.

Do not build:

- AI that speaks at length but performs no work;
- AI that analyzes but does not help decide;
- dashboards that look smart but do not help buyers pay or avoid loss;
- complex AI workflows without paid evidence;
- automation for things nobody wants to buy;
- technical beauty that does not reduce buyer loss.

Test question:

Would a real user pay for this result?

If not, return to buyer pain.

---

## 11. Anti-Manipulation And Human Strength

AI always influences humans. The question is not whether influence exists.
The question is whether the influence makes the user stronger or weaker.

Good AI should make users:

- clearer;
- more capable;
- better informed;
- aware of risks;
- able to refuse;
- able to reverse;
- able to see evidence;
- able to make better decisions;
- less exposed to avoidable loss.

Bad AI:

- hides intent;
- creates pressure;
- exploits fear, greed, loneliness, confusion, or status anxiety;
- pushes the user toward the system's goal;
- narrows choices;
- makes the user dependent without understanding more.

If the user becomes weaker after the interaction, the feature is wrong.

---

## 12. Human Strength Test

Every AI workflow must pass this test:

1. Does the user understand more clearly?
2. Does the user have more real choices?
3. Does the user see evidence?
4. Does the user know the risks?
5. Can the user stop?
6. Can the user edit?
7. Can the user challenge the recommendation?
8. Does AI mark uncertainty?
9. Does AI avoid exploiting weak emotions?
10. After the interaction, is the user stronger or more dependent?

If the user becomes weaker, the feature is wrong.

---

## 13. The Goal Is Not User Control

AI must not become a system for controlling users.

AI should help humans:

- observe better;
- decide better;
- correct mistakes faster;
- avoid losing money;
- avoid being deceived;
- compare choices;
- control commitments;
- increase capability.

This product does not replace human judgment.
It reduces noise and preserves decision control.

---

## 14. Architecture Must Serve Cashflow

Build order:

1. Paid proof workflow
2. Evidence ledger
3. AI sourcing and comparison
4. Human approval checkpoint
5. Risk scoring
6. Billing by delivered work
7. Operator dashboard
8. Automation
9. Marketplace
10. Scale

Do not reverse this order without a strong reason.

The biggest mistake is building marketplace, dashboard, or automation before a user proves willingness to pay for a small valuable unit of work.

---

## 15. Coding Agent Integrity Check

Before coding, every agent must answer internally:

1. What buyer loss does this reduce?
2. Is there a real payer?
3. Is there a natural payment trigger?
4. Does it produce evidence?
5. What real work does AI perform?
6. Where does human approval happen?
7. What is the risk if automation is wrong?
8. Is there a manual-first path?
9. Can this prove paid value in a small workflow?
10. Does it cause product drift?

If unclear, choose the smallest manual-first implementation that can produce evidence and paid proof. Do not code broadly.

---

## 16. Anti-Over-Architecture

The founder has a large system vision, but the product must move MVP-first.

Prefer:

- small but real;
- manual but paid;
- simple but evidenced;
- few features but real loss reduction;
- one paid workflow before expanding the system.

Do not prioritize:

- large architecture before product-market fit;
- many modules before paid proof;
- automation before workflow truth;
- complex AI before buyer demand;
- marketplace before trust;
- dashboard before economic decisions.

---

## 17. Six-Part Workflow Contract

Every important workflow should include six parts:

1. Goal - what the user wants to achieve.
2. Loss - what the user avoids losing.
3. AI work - what labor AI performs.
4. Evidence - what proof is stored.
5. Human approval - what the human approves.
6. Paid unit - what value unit can be charged.

If one part is missing, the workflow is not mature enough.

---

## 18. Definition Of Success

Success is not:

- many features;
- complex code;
- impressive AI prose;
- beautiful UI;
- large dashboards;
- many agents;
- modern architecture theater.

Success is:

- real users;
- real pain;
- real money;
- real evidence;
- better decisions;
- reduced loss;
- AI doing real labor;
- correct human approval;
- repeatable and scalable delivered work.
