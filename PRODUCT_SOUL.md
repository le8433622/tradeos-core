# PRODUCT_SOUL.md - The Soul Of The Product

Canonical source: `FOUNDER_THINKING.md`.

Product soul sentence:

This product helps buyers avoid loss and make better economic decisions by letting AI do the labor, preserving evidence, and keeping humans in control of commitment.

Core doctrine:

AI does the labor.
Human approves the commitment.
Evidence proves the work.
Cashflow validates the product.
Human nature is the invariant behavioral dataset.

## 1. This Project Exists Because Most Software Does Not Solve Real Business Pain

Most software manages data.
This product must improve decisions.

Most AI apps talk.
This product must do work.

Most dashboards display.
This product must reduce loss.

Most marketplaces list providers.
This product must create trust, evidence, and accountable transactions.

---

## 2. Core Belief

Money is the final test.

Knowledge, intelligence, strategy, and AI are only useful if they lead to:

- real value;
- real buyer payment;
- real saved cost;
- real reduced risk;
- real accountable outcomes.

If the system looks intelligent but does not create buyer value, it fails.

If the system creates buyer value but looks simple, it can still be correct.

---

## 3. Buyer Value First

The product must start from buyer loss, not feature imagination.

Loss types:

- money loss;
- time loss;
- trust loss;
- status loss;
- safety loss;
- control loss;
- convenience loss;
- opportunity loss;
- relationship loss;
- operational continuity loss.

Each feature must map to a loss type.

Start from:

- what the buyer is losing;
- what the buyer fears;
- where the buyer is stuck;
- whether the buyer would pay to avoid that loss.

Use stable human nature as the behavioral baseline: wanting gain, fearing loss, confusion, pride, doubt, distrust, urgency, status pressure, and desire for control. Sandbox scenarios can be valid when they model these timeless patterns honestly.

Do not start from AI features, dashboards, marketplace supply, or generic workflow imagination.

---

## 4. AI As Economic Operator

AI is valuable when it converts messy options into clear decisions.

The AI should help answer:

- What should I buy?
- From whom?
- At what price?
- With what risk?
- What proof do we have?
- What should be negotiated?
- What should be rejected?
- What should a human approve?

The AI must not be a vague assistant.
It must be a decision layer.

AI should perform labor that is repetitive, time-consuming, and information-noisy. The human should keep high-value judgment and commitment control.

---

## 5. Delivered Work Over Subscription Fantasy

Users should pay for completed value units, not vague promises.

Possible paid work units:

- paid sourcing request;
- paid supplier verification;
- paid quote collection;
- paid comparison table;
- paid negotiation batch;
- paid risk report;
- paid booking/order hold;
- paid dispute support;
- paid reorder optimization.

Every paid unit must produce evidence.

If completed work cannot be evidenced, the system should not demand payment for it.

---

## 6. Evidence Is Trust

Trust is not a slogan.
Trust is recorded work.

The system should preserve:

- what was done;
- why it was done;
- who approved;
- what data was used;
- what uncertainty remains;
- what risk was accepted;
- what result occurred.

Evidence reduces disputes.
Evidence supports billing.
Evidence improves future intelligence.

Evidence should preserve the process, not only the final result.

---

## 7. Human Control Is Not Optional

The AI may search, compare, negotiate, draft, and recommend.

But the human must approve:

- money movement;
- legal commitment;
- final order;
- irreversible action;
- high-risk decision.

The goal is not to replace human control.
The goal is to remove low-value labor while preserving high-value judgment.

Money, legal commitment, final orders, irreversible actions, and high-risk decisions require human approval.

---

## 8. Do Not Build Generic Software

This project must not become:

- generic CRM;
- generic ERP;
- generic marketplace;
- generic chatbot;
- generic dashboard;
- generic procurement tool.

It must remain:

- buyer-value-first;
- AI-labor-driven;
- evidence-backed;
- human-approved;
- cashflow-oriented;
- trust-centered.

---

## 9. Manual First, Then Automation

Do not automate what is not understood manually.

Correct sequence:

1. Make the manual workflow work.
2. Capture evidence.
3. Find repeatable patterns.
4. Standardize registered actions.
5. Let AI orchestrate only existing safe actions.

Manual workflow is the source of truth. Automation is the orchestration layer.

---

## 10. Platform Access Is Leverage, Not Oxygen

The system must survive without one platform API.

If API access is blocked, reroute through user-provided links, screenshots, uploaded invoices, manual catalogs, chat quotes, operator-entered data, browser workflow, email, Zalo, WhatsApp, or alternate sources.

---

## 11. Six-Part Workflow Contract

Every important workflow should include:

1. Goal - what the user wants to achieve.
2. Loss - what the user avoids losing.
3. AI work - what labor AI performs.
4. Evidence - what proof is stored.
5. Human approval - what commitment human approves.
6. Paid unit - what delivered-work unit can be charged.

If one part is missing, the workflow is not mature enough.

---

## 12. Final Rule

If a feature does not create real buyer value, real evidence, real control, or real cashflow, do not build it.
